# Search as a teacher, the follow-up: design (2026-10-12)

**Status: a design by the operator, for the owner. It registers nothing. No code was written for it, no game was played, nothing ran on the rig. Every step named here needs the owner's decision and its own ledger entry before it runs, and nothing here trains. Codex reviewed it twice before it was committed (`.codex-reviews/d486-review.md`, `.codex-reviews/d486-review2.md`); the first review's five blocking findings changed the design and are listed at the end.**

How to read the labels: **[ledger]** read from `DECISIONS.md` or a doc in this repository (the repository's own record, not re-measured here); **[code]** read from source; **[estimate]** planning extrapolations from labelled inputs, whose error bounds are unmeasured; **[hypothesis]** the operator's judgment, not verified.

## The question

The eight rungs read since chain 58 do not show consistent climbing against the held-out opponents, and search at play time is the one thing that read Positive there. Can search be turned into (a) a stronger bot at play time on the current best checkpoint and (b) a training signal, on one rig and no cloud?

## What is known

1. **Eight rungs, one above its parent.** Of the eight rungs read from chain 58 and chain 63 under the standard pool rotation, one was above its parent against chain 37 and chain 46 (chain 63), three were below and four had at least one interval containing zero; all eight beat the parent head to head by +47.2 to +95.1. That is a descriptive count across entries with different blocks and loads [ledger: D485]. Chain 63 is the warm start and no candidate stands beside it.
2. **The search seat reads Positive, on another checkpoint.** Chain 55 + m1 with the seat (up to 4 candidates, 16 rollouts each, stopping at the end of the own team turn, the end of the match or a 200-step cutoff; shaped return plus the value head; deviate only above 0.10 and two floored standard errors, the paired-difference standard error being floored by the pooled error of a candidate mean, as D434 describes) was +45.5 [+28.3, +63.4] and +45.3 [+27.2, +64.5] over the same checkpoint without it against chain 37 and chain 46, at 48 times the cost of plain play [ledger: D434]. It had positive paired margins over the checkpoint at temperatures 0.75, 0.5 and 0.25 against both opponents on the same seeds; the margin over temperature 0.5 against chain 37 is +24.2 [+2.7, +45.3], and any claim that rests on that margin needs a fresh-seed replay [ledger: D442].
3. **The seat deviates rarely, to actions the policy almost never samples.** About 2.6 deviations a game, 1.3% of searched decisions [ledger: D434]. In the exploratory screens, 25 of 28 chosen alternatives had a policy probability below 0.001, by the agent's report of the raw records [ledger: `docs/search-probe-2026-10-07/FINDINGS.md`]. At strongly saturated decisions, direct policy and entropy gradients can be extremely small, with some terms disappearing through floating-point rounding or underflow. The exploration study's review does not support a blanket claim of zero learning signal or zero entropy gradient [ledger: `docs/plans/exploration-in-training-design-2026-10-07.md`, section 8].
4. **The cheapest student did not fit.** Test 1 trained only the 454 policy rows of the output layer of chain 55 toward 370 labels with a KL term at 65,430 other decisions. The largest dose put 0.605 on its training labels against a registered 0.80 and the plan stopped; no gate was played and no recipe was closed [ledger: D449]. In the exploratory extensions at lambda 4 and 16, increasing the run to 24,000 steps did not materially improve training label probability (0.590 to 0.608 at lambda 16, 0.417 to 0.435 at lambda 4). Validation label probability remained substantially below training probability on 77 labels (about 0.13 to 0.18 at lambda 16, 0.10 to 0.12 at lambda 4) [ledger: `docs/search-distill-2026-10-08/NOTES.md`, section 0000; exploratory, decides nothing]. This weakens the case for simply extending those runs. It does not establish a feature-capacity limit, close R1, or measure match strength.
5. **The accepted whole-game search-seat evidence is on chain 55 under m1.** Chain 63 is another policy: lambda 0.99, played plain, about 3.3 to 4.0 activations a team turn in its recent gates against chain 55 + m1's 6.4 to 6.6, an explained variance in training of 0.864 against 0.915 for chain 68 and chain 70 (a descriptive training diagnostic, no measure of the critic's accuracy where the search reads it), and no effect of m1 shown on it (+14.0 [0.0, +28.0]) [ledger: D434, D465, D484, D485]. D434 says that using the seat on another checkpoint needs that checkpoint's own gate.
6. **Labels are slow with the seat as built.** The label tool took 88.5 elapsed seconds a game of one process for 30 screened roots a game (the smoke's rate) and found 0.566 deviation labels a game [ledger: D444, D445]. A fully searched game took about 364 elapsed seconds on a droplet for about 2.6 deviations [ledger: D434]. The chain 55 measurements imply roughly 140 to 160 elapsed worker-seconds per deviation label, depending on the route, and the two routes do not produce identical label artifacts. At twelve workers and the implied 140 to 160 elapsed worker-seconds per label, the projection is about 6,500 to 7,400 labels a day; an optimistic 100-second assumption would raise it to about 10,400 a day. Neither rate has been measured on the rig or for chain 63 [estimate]. The operator read `nproc` = 16 on the rig; sixteen logical processors do not establish twelve independent cores of capacity.
7. **No cloud.** By the owner's instruction of 2026-10-09 as the operator records it, all compute is the rig and the Mac keeps the ledger, scoring and reviews and plays no games. Test 1's label and dataset tools ran on droplets [ledger: D444, D445]; a rig replacement has to be designed, not assumed.
8. **Where the rollouts' time goes is not measured.** A searched decision is about 59 rollouts (D434: 36.6 million rollouts over 619,000 searched decisions against each opponent). The tournament search path handles one real game at a time per worker on the CPU, while the rollout core already batches inference over active candidate rollouts and both observation views [code: `play_harness/search.py` at `5ab3ab6`]. Native stepping, sampling, observation construction and a GPU backend may reduce its cost; the saving is unmeasured. The trainer's native environment makes about 99,000 to 100,000 steps a second on the same rig with nothing beside it [ledger: D484]; that is no measurement of a search kernel's throughput. A C rollout kernel is the item the design study parked first, with "halves the cost or better" as a hypothesis [ledger: `docs/plans/search-probe-design-2026-10-07.md`, section 5].
9. **The seat as built refuses to play without a mask, and the rig runner does not carry search settings.** `SearchSeat.__init__` raises on empty masks ("a search seat plays under at least one action mask (the measured setting is m1)"), `tournament.player_specs` refuses a search player without a mask, and the droplet runner's `search_refusal` requires a mask and one game per worker [code: `play_harness/search.py`, `play_harness/tournament.py`, `tools/droplet_tournament.py` at `5ab3ab6`]. The rig runner's `shard_spec` forwards masks and ignores a player's search settings and temperature, its call to `tournament_argv` passes no search argument, and `cmd_collect` calls `verify_run` without the expected search mapping [code: `docs/rig-gates-2026-10-09/rig_gate.py`]. So today a plan with a searched player would be played plain by the rig runner, and a temperature arm at the default temperature.

## Where the plain plan ("distil the search") is wrong, and what it misses

1. **It skips the teacher's whole-game evidence on the checkpoint that matters.** No search has been played on chain 63. Without an established gain on chain 63, this setting lacks the whole-game evidence needed to justify it as the current teacher. A non-Positive result would not prove that every label is useless or that every student must fail.
2. **"Run the same fit longer" is a weak next student.** Item 4 above. A follow-up must distinguish optimization and preservation-loss trade-offs from coverage and representation limits; the existing accepted labels allow cheap diagnostics of that under a later authorization, with the test and reserve games kept locked.
3. **Rollout cost is a major constraint, and how binding it is has not been measured.** It limits new label collection and repeated search gates on one rig. Whether it dominates teacher quality, student fit, the preservation trade-off, trajectory coverage or distribution shift is not known. The first things to establish are the current teacher's utility and where the seat's time goes; a kernel is worth building only if the expected saving justifies its engineering cost.
4. **It forgets that search can be useful with no student at all.** A superhuman bot may search at play time; the roadmap says so [ledger: `docs/plans/superhuman-roadmap-2026-10-03.md`, item 4 and M2]. The design study estimated that this search might fit human-facing latency budgets; latency on the rig and at larger budgets remains to be measured.
5. **The evaluator is the shaped reward plus the value head.** The gates show that acting on it wins more games against two older relatives. They do not show that it is right at any single decision or against a stronger or foreign opponent [ledger: D434's limits, which this design carries].

## The ladder proposed

Each step needs the owner's decision and its own entry, with its own plan and review. No step is started by this page.

**Step 0. Does the seat lift chain 63? (a gate; nothing trains; idle rig time; it needs a small reviewed code change first)**

Two forms, and the owner chooses:

- **Form A, the measured setting (the operator's recommendation).** S = chain 63 + m1 + the seat at D430's setting; C1 = chain 63 + m1; C0 = chain 63 plain; T = chain 63 + m1 at per-head temperature 0.5; each against chain 37 and chain 46 on shared fresh seeds, both legs, with D430's identity pair (the seat with delta infinity must reproduce C1's games exactly). The seat's code is unchanged: this is the setting D434 measured, on another checkpoint. It answers "does the seat add to chain 63 under m1" (S minus C1) and, beside it, "is the searched player better than the plain checkpoint the ladder uses" (S minus C0) and "is it better than per-head temperature 0.5 on chain 63 against these two opponents" (S minus T).
- **Form B, an unmasked seat.** S = chain 63 with the seat and no mask against C0 and a plain temperature arm. It asks the question on the policy as the ladder plays it. It is a new seat behaviour and no compatibility change. Without m1, legal END_TURN actions would remain in the turn-level support and could enter the candidate set under the existing candidate rule. The accepted whole-game search-seat evidence excludes this choice; the exploration study includes an unmasked END_TURN rollout probe, and that does not establish an unmasked searched player's whole-game utility. The seat refuses empty masks on purpose. Form B needs an unmasked seat implementation with its own identity and no-peeking tests and corresponding changes to tournament validation, search acceptance and any reused runner refusal checks, while preserving the measured m1 behaviour, and a registered pilot before a gate.

For either form:
- **Code needed before any game:** the rig runner must forward the registered search settings and temperatures and verify the expected search settings at collection; search stays on the unbatched path (one game per worker) and so do its matched controls. Collection must receive the expected search mapping for each shard, the identity player's infinite delta included, and the temperature arm must be accepted against the registration by the gate and search acceptance tools, whose expectations must agree; forwarding a temperature does not by itself establish acceptance of the intended arm. Form B also needs the changes named above. The revised code and acceptance tools are reviewed, pinned and hashed before the gate. No change to the search algorithm is intended in Form A.
- **Reading, D430's form, to be written out in the registration:** Unread for incomplete, invalid, integrity-failing or undefined evidence; Negative if either seat-minus-control interval is entirely below zero; Positive if both point estimates exceed +20 and both intervals are entirely above zero; Flat if both intervals lie wholly within -20 to +20; Inconclusive otherwise. The registration must say separately how S minus T and S minus C0 are read and whether either controls further work.
- **Size.** At 1,600 games a pair (800 seeds, both legs, as D425 used) Form A is eight pairs and 20 identity games, 12,820 games, 3,220 of them searched. Scaling D434's contrast half-widths from 3,200 to 1,600 games gives roughly plus or minus 25 to 26 Elo: a gain near +45 could clear, a gain near +20 to +25 may stay Inconclusive, and Flat will often be out of reach at that width. It is a fixed-cost screen. The registration chooses the size by the smallest gain worth having and accepts the fixed-look consequence.
- **Time.** 3,220 searched games at 150 to 364 elapsed seconds of one worker over six to eight workers is about 17 to 54 hours [estimate]. That covers the searched games only; plain games, setup, scoring, queue imbalance and retries are extra, and scaling from chain 55's rate by activations alone is weak. An authorized smoke measures the rate before the gate is committed.
- **The registration must also fix:** the checkpoint blob and lineage-sidecar, reward manifest, runtime, source, runner and acceptance-tool identities by hash; kick-off starts and natural match completion; the fresh seed block and separate smoke seeds; the identity sample (20 games, its opponent, seeds, compared fields, exact match); the complete search settings and opponent-model setting; one-look discipline and undefined-statistic handling; D430's real-game and rollout integrity checks, its cap-rejection ceiling and its explicit exclusion of the shim's unexported reward telemetry counters; the shard order, the restart and replacement rules and what completed work is kept (the rig runner queues whole-pair shards one after another and has no recovery policy of its own; D470's host restart is the exposure for a queue of one to two days).
- **Consequence.** This page adopts nothing. A Positive Step 0 would establish a measured gain over its control against these two opponents and would support this searched player as a candidate teacher; it would not validate every label or show that a student can learn it. Its own registration must state whether a Positive makes the searched player a play-time option, as D434 did for chain 55 + m1, or only permits a later proposal. Anything else stops this ladder at the present seat setting; it would not decide the value of other evaluators, settings or student recipes.
- **The operator's guess, written before anything is played:** Form A Positive on S minus C1 about 50 in 100 [hypothesis].

**Step 1. Where does the seat's time go, and is a kernel worth building? (a profile and a bounded feasibility assessment; nothing trains)**
- Profile the seat on the rig (engine stepping, sampling, observation construction, forwards, Python overhead) during Step 0's smoke or beside it, and write a bounded assessment of a native rollout kernel: what it would replace, the expected saving, the tests.
- If a kernel is built, it targets exact behavioural parity with the present seat: candidates, per-rollout actions and random-number mapping, reward, stop and bootstrap components, gain and standard-error arithmetic and the selected deviations, with the no-peeking, state-isolation, support, recurrence and identity checks kept. If numerical or random-number differences prevent parity, those differences are characterized and the changed implementation needs its own gate before use; a gate re-validates a changed implementation and does not establish parity.
- If it achieves a material speed-up, it reduces the compute cost of later search gates and label collection. How much is a hypothesis; qualifying it on the rig's GPU takes rig time.

**Step 2. Optional: what a larger budget buys at play time (gates; nothing trains)**
- With a cheaper seat, the searched player at two or three rollout budgets against the two held-out opponents. It tests whether larger budgets improve this searched player at an acceptable cost. More rollouts can also optimize an imperfect evaluator more confidently, so a gain is not assumed, and it is no prerequisite for a student.

**Step 3. A student (the first step that would train anything; a new student is designed after Step 0 is read)**
- Cheap first, and not dependent on Step 0: diagnostics on the accepted Test 1 labels (what limits the fit: the optimizer, the preservation term, or the features), under their own authorization, with the test and reserve games locked. They may run alongside Step 0's preparation or play.
- New labels, if a student is pursued: from the chosen checkpoint and setting, made on the rig, with the returns of all candidates stored. The label count is set by a stated rule.
- The student is not chosen here. Three candidates, with what each must answer:
  - *The whole policy path trained offline* (Test 1's postponed R2): window placement, early-match history, reference recurrent states, multiplicities and duplicate weighting, a drift bound, the value anchor's normalisation and local value errors must be specified first [ledger: `docs/search-distill-2026-10-08/PLAN.md`, section 9].
  - *A separate small head that imitates the seat's rule.* It would not have to move saturated policy logits and would leave the checkpoint unchanged. Whether the trunk's features carry the needed information is a risk, not something Test 1 established, and its cost at play and its feature interface need measuring.
  - *A term in the trainer.* It would integrate the teacher signal directly into later PPO rungs and needs a trainer change, a new build and a matched control. Offline policy updates could also feed later rungs under a separately designed lineage and continuation contract.
- Reading: gate the student beside its unchanged checkpoint against both opponents, as Test 1 registered. A contemporaneous comparison with the searched teacher would be new and must specify compatible inference settings.

**Outside this ladder, and needed before any claim about a superhuman bot:** stronger or independent opponents in a later evaluation design. Chain 37 and chain 46 are older relatives of every checkpoint here.

## What I recommend, and what needs the owner's word

1. **Step 0, Form A, if the owner chooses this direction.** It reuses the measured seat unchanged, costs no money and adopts nothing by itself. It needs the small rig-runner change above, an identity proof and a smoke, all under the owner's decision and their own reviewed entry. I can prepare the written registration proposal and the compatibility assessment without it; implementation and smoke games wait for the decision.
2. **Step 1's profile with Step 0's smoke; a kernel only if the profile supports it.**
3. **No new student before Step 0 is read.** My odds for a student of the Test 1 kind at a few thousand labels reading Positive at a gate are about 15 in 100 [hypothesis].
4. **Not proposed here:** any training rung, any change to the recipe, the pool or lambda, any play-time default.

## Limits

- One seat setting, chosen on exploratory runs on another checkpoint (D425's limits). Step 0 tests that setting on chain 63 and no other.
- Chain 37 and chain 46 are older relatives of every checkpoint here. Nothing on this ladder measures strength against a foreign opponent or a person.
- The time estimates are scaled from droplet measurements of another checkpoint under m1; the rig's rate is not measured.
- Any speed-up from a kernel is a hypothesis.
- The odds written here are judgments.

## What the first review changed

The first draft said Step 0 needed no new code in the seat and proposed an unmasked seat; that the trainer gives no signal at saturated decisions; that the exploratory long fine-tune showed the features did not carry the labels; that the cost of a rollout is the binding constraint, with a kernel and larger budgets ahead of any student; that a trainer term is the only student that compounds; and that the operator would prepare and register Step 0 unless the owner said otherwise. The reviewer found the first false in the source (the seat refuses empty masks, the rig runner drops search settings and temperatures), the next four stronger than their sources, and disagreed with treating the owner's silence as permission for code changes and smoke games. All are corrected above: Step 0 has two forms and a named code change, the mechanistic and student claims are narrowed to what the sources say, the kernel is behind a profile, larger budgets are optional, and every step waits for the owner. The second review found all five closed and no new blocking defect, and gave six further corrections, applied above: what an unmasked seat would and would not add to the END_TURN evidence, the other mask guards Form B must change, the stop rule and the standard-error floor in the setting's summary, the sidecar, completion and telemetry items of the registration checklist, the label projection's optimistic endpoint, and the error bound claimed for estimates. Its remaining difference, that the cheap diagnostics on existing labels need not wait for Step 0, is taken.
