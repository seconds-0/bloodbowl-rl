# Trainer review and long-run build, 2026-10-02

The owner asked for a rigorous review and optimization of the training code before a long run on the RTX 2070 rig. This page records what was reviewed, what was found, what was adopted, and what was deliberately left alone. The decision record is D407 in `DECISIONS.md`.

## How the review ran

- Six reviewers, one per dimension, read the exact patched trainer tree that ran chain 36 (a copy of the rig's `vendor/PufferLib`, whose key files hash the same as the rig's), the launchers, and the rig's logs and scripts (read-only).
- Each reviewer's findings then went to a separate verifier whose job was to refute them from primary evidence. 48 findings came back; the verifier column below is that second read. "PARTLY" means the mechanism held and the size or the conclusion did not.
- Nothing in this table was adopted on a reviewer's word. The adopted items were measured on the rig (next section).

## What was adopted, with the measurements

The long-run build is `origin/main` plus three changes. All three were already written and reviewed in September and had never been installed together on the rig.

1. **Blitz reachability fast path** (branch `fix/blitz-reachability-fastpath-v6-20260912`, merged). An early-exit reachability query for Blitz declarations.
2. **Scripted-bank forward skip** (`training/puffer_skip_scripted_bank_forward.patch`, opt-in). The contact bot plays every seat of bank tag 4, and the trainer still ran a full policy forward for those rows at every step and discarded it.
3. **Deciding-row loss telemetry** (`training/puffer_deciding_row_telemetry.patch`, opt-in). Full-precision losses, grad norm, explained variance and deciding-row kl and entropy in a `PUFFER_LOSS_JSON` line. No effect on training.

## Findings that change how past results read

- **The time split was misread.** The dashboard's "Forward" row is the train step's forward and backward, not the rollout's. Per 1,465 ms epoch on chain 36: rollout GPU about 619 ms, env about 180 ms, train about 604 ms. The recipe is bound by the GPU in both phases.
- **Rollout GPU time is a latency chain per opponent bank**, about 252 ms plus about 86 ms for each frozen-bank forward, almost independent of how many rows each forward carries. That is why skipping one bank's forward is worth 5 to 6% and why half precision would not help.
- **The entropy coefficient never annealed.** Under CUDA graphs the annealed value is passed by value into a captured kernel, so the value at capture is replayed for the whole run. Every native run in this project trained at a constant entropy coefficient (0.009 on the current recipe). Entries that read as entropy or schedule results (D261, D259, D271, D248, D252) were at constant coefficients. The verifier found the same in the torch backend. This run does not fix it: a live anneal would cut the end-of-rung entropy bonus about ninefold, which is an algorithm change that needs its own arm.
- **Each warm restart resets the learning rate to full.** A warm start loads weights only. In the three same-recipe continuation rungs (chains 34, 35, 36) the in-run score against every frozen bank drops within the first 25 to 50M steps and returns to the parent's level only at 2.1 to 3.0B steps; the net change against the scripted bot per rung is about zero. The logs cannot tell "damage, then relearn" from "this score follows the current learning rate", and head-to-head strength did rise from chain 30 to 34. This is the question the first two rungs of the long run test (D407).
- **The newest self is never an opponent.** The pool appends the warm checkpoint last, and the scripted bot plays that bank's seats, so the learner meets its immediate predecessor only in the 4.7% mirror games. About a quarter of frozen games go to an anchor the learner beats nine times in ten. Recorded, not changed.
- **"Replay ratio 1.0" is about 1.9 draws per learner row per epoch**, because the minibatch count is computed from all rows and the sampler draws only learner rows. The 0.25, 1.0 and 2.0 arms were about 0.48, 1.9 and 3.8.
- **Training seeds 42 and 44 share environment streams.** Env seeds are base seed plus env index, so the two runs share 1,022 of 1,024 per-slot roster and kickoff sequences. Policy sampling and learning still differ, so the replicates remain valid for training noise. Future replicate seeds should sit at least 1,024 apart.
- **The exam wrapper could not fail.** `rig_exam.sh` ignores every cell's exit status and always prints its done marker. The chain 35 and 36 exams were checked by hand (all cells rc=0). The long run's exam runs inside the stage script and fails closed.

Checked and found correct: GAE alignment, terminal handling and two-seat routing; frozen rows excluded from sampling, advantage normalization and priorities; joint log-prob, masks and entropy recomputed consistently between rollout and train; recurrent state handling (the suspected rollout-versus-train mismatch does not exist); host transfers already pinned and asynchronous; VRAM flat; throughput flat over 9.5 hours. `max_grad_norm` 1.5 against a 1.82 median norm is close to inert under Muon and not worth an arm.

## Left alone on purpose

- **Forking bank forwards onto parallel streams** (upper bound about +23%, supported range 0 to about 200 ms per epoch, nothing measured). The largest remaining throughput idea. It needs an nsys trace first and is hours of CUDA work.
- **Half precision** for the train step (bounded 0 to +25%, a numerics change) and for frozen-bank inference (under 3%).
- **Thread count and pinning** (unmeasured; 17 busy threads on 16 logical cores, nothing pinned). A zero-build probe set for a quiet box.
- **Train-path cleanup bundle** (+1.5 to 2.4%).
- **Mid-rung resume and shorter rungs.** Worth about an hour in expectation over five days; the on-box supervisor is the larger fix.

## All findings

### Rollout (policy forward) throughput

I read the rollout path end to end in vendor/PufferLib/src (pufferlib.cu, models.cu, kernels.cu, vecenv.h, bindings.cu), the skip-scripted patch and its rig verification doc, and analysed 2,937 epochs of the chain 36 log on the rig (read-only). Headline: the brief's time split is misread. "Forward 581 ms" is the Train forward/backward (Train 613 = Misc 32 + Forward 581), not part of Evaluate. The rollout is not FLOP-bound: its GPU time is a serial latency chain of about 252 ms per epoch plus about 86 ms for every frozen-bank forward, almost independent of row count (rig grid: 252 / 333 / 598 / 852 ms at 0 / 1 / 4 / 8 banks with total rows fixed). Env time is not hidden behind the GPU either: across epochs GPU wait is flat against env time (slope -0.01) while Evaluate rises 1.22 ms per env ms. So the levers are fewer serial bank chains (the skip patch, then forking the bank forwards onto parallel streams inside the captured graph) and a faster env, not FP16 and not a different buffer split. Confidence is high on the diagnosis (measured on the rig) and medium on the size of the fork/join gain (not yet built or measured).

### Train step throughput

I read the train path end to end in W/vendor/PufferLib (src/pufferlib.cu train_impl and loss/prio/GAE kernels, src/models.cu, src/kernels.cu, src/muon.cu, src/bindings.cu, pufferlib/pufferl.py, selfplay.py), the update-budget and SPS scope docs, D396-D406, and sampled the chain 36 log and manifest on the rig read-only. Headline: the train step is fp32-GEMM-bound, not wasteful. One step is 89 cuBLAS GEMMs totalling about 395 GFLOP in 71.75 ms, which is about 5.5 TFLOPS on a card whose fp32 ceiling is about 7.5, so trace-identical changes can recover only about 2 to 3% of the epoch from the train phase. Frozen-bank rows are excluded before any gather or forward, per-minibatch GAE and priority recompute is cheap and semantically required, and VRAM is statically allocated and measured flat at 6.4/8 GB. The only large train-phase lever is FP16 tensor-core GEMM, which is a numerics change and not something to put in this launch. I also found a host memory leak (pufferl.py all_logs, measured +0.064 GB/h RSS) that is harmless for 9.5 h rungs but would hit the 12 GB WSL2 cap in a single process after about a week. Confidence is high on structure and measured numbers, medium on per-component time shares, which come from a FLOP model and not a profiler.

### Algorithm correctness

I read pufferlib.cu end to end, muon.cu, kernels.cu, the MinGRU and policy code in models.cu, the rollout loop in vecenv.h, bindings.cu, pufferl.py, selfplay.py, binding.c and the c_step/terminal/reset paths of bloodbowl.h, then checked the claims against the chain 30/34/35/36 logs on the rig (read-only). Headline: the entropy-coefficient anneal has never run on the native trainer. `ent_coef` is a by-value kernel argument frozen into the captured train CUDA graph, and the chain 36 loss panels give an implied coefficient of 0.0089 at rung start and 0.0091 in the last 12% of the rung, where the schedule says about 0.0010. Second: every warm restart costs a measurable dip (in-training score drops 0.02-0.04 within 16-47M steps and takes 0.6B to 2B steps to come back), which the ledger knows qualitatively (D245/D248/D252) but has not priced on the current recipe. The rest of the trainer checked out: GAE alignment, frozen-row exclusion, exact joint log-prob recomputation and recurrent-state handling are correct, and the recurrent-state mismatch in the brief does not exist. Confidence is high on the entropy bug (code plus measurement), medium on what to do about the LR sawtooth; nothing here has had a Codex review.
