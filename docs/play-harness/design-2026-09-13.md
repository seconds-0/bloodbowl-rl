# Play harness: human vs RL policy (design, phase A)

Status 2026-09-13. Phase A delivers a design for sign-off plus a headless backend
spike. The browser client is not built. Branch `feat/play-harness-20260913`.

Deliverables in this branch:

- `play_harness/`: engine shim, CPU policy seat, game session JSON API, scripted
  drivers, parity fixture format, bench, tests.
- `docs/play-harness/mockups/`: five static screens (HTML) and PNG renders at
  1440x900 and 400 px wide under `png/`.

## 1. Goals and constraints

- Alex plays full BB2025 matches against a trained checkpoint (default chain 25,
  sha `109c55d3`) and turns his reactions into data: game records, flagged bot
  moves with the policy's state, and a post-game survey.
- The bot must play the policy the exams measured. That means native evaluation
  recurrence: one policy forward per env `c_step`, state zeroed once when a match
  starts, sampling from exact joint support (D218, D388).
- Local only: the server binds 127.0.0.1. A later `tailscale serve` is optional.
  Never public. BBTV services (bbstream, bbweb, bbtv-tunnel) are not touched.
- No changes to `puffer/bloodbowl/` or `engine/`. The spike needed none.

## 2. Research summary

### FUMBBL FFB client conventions (christerk/ffb, vendored read-only)

Kept, because players already know them:

- 26x15 landscape pitch, home red on the left, away blue on the right, stone and
  cloth sidebars, team colours in the log.
- Player tokens with overlays for state rather than a separate view; used players
  faded.
- Yellow move squares with target numbers. A square needing Rush and Dodge is
  split diagonally with Rush top left and Dodge bottom right.
- Block indicators: small dice squares on the target, green when you pick, red
  when the opponent picks.
- Pass range bands: Quick green, Short yellow, Long red, Long Bomb black.
- Push arrows on legal push squares. One End Turn button whose label follows the
  phase (End Setup, Kick Off, End Turn).
- Illegal input is hidden (no menu item, no square), not rejected with a message.

Changed, because they are known pain points (forum threads and source review):

- The turn clock lives in the window title in FFB. Here it is in the scoreboard.
- FFB dialogs centre on the pitch and cover the action. Here dialogs anchor next
  to the target and can be hidden with H; on a phone they become a bottom sheet.
- FFB block colours disagree between the target marker and the dialog, and no
  words say who picks. Here the dialog header says "You pick" or "Bot picks".
- Tackle zones are off by default in FFB. Here the opponent's zones show while a
  player is selected.
- Used players are hard to tell apart in FFB. Here they fade and carry a check.
- Prone and stunned overlays "take getting used to". Here a lying token carries a
  DOWN or STUN tag.
- Kick-off results and turnovers only appear in the FFB log. Here they get a
  banner (not mocked).

### BBTV today

`stream/web/app.js` draws a 936x540 canvas (36 px squares) from protocol-v1
spectator messages. `stream_backend/game.py` runs both sides through the torch
Puffer backend and chooses each head independently against the marginal mask,
which is outside D218 and would abort a current obs-v6 env. It is spectator-only:
no legal-action surface, no act message. Reusable: drawing conventions, skill
names (`stream/web/skills.json`), roster names and the exact icon identity
mapping (`tools/stage_spectator_art.py` keyed by `team_id` and `position_id`).

### Art licensing

The five PNGs in `stream/web/art` are byte-identical to FFB resources, which are
FUMBBL's icon cache. The FFB MIT licence covers the software; the icon library
page states FUMBBL retains ownership of the images. For a private local harness
the practical risk is low if icons load from the gitignored `vendor/ffb` folder
and are never republished. Anything public needs written permission or original
art. The mockups use original abstract tokens (disc, position shorthand, jersey
number), which is also FFB's own "Abstract icons" mode, so nothing FUMBBL-owned is
committed by this branch.

## 3. Interaction model

Principles:

1. **The UI never builds engine actions.** Every state version comes with the
   engine's legal list, annotated for the UI. A click resolves to an action id
   from that list. Anything not in the list has no square, no menu item, no
   button. The server re-checks the id against the current version and the shim
   re-checks the tuple against exact joint support before `c_step`, so a stale
   or forged request is refused without touching the match (c_step itself would
   abort the process).
2. **Lookahead instead of cancel.** Some engine steps commit early (ACTIVATE has
   no cancel). The server peeks the next legal set on a scratch copy of the
   session (`bbp_peek_legal`): no dice consumed, no policy forward. The player
   menu is built from that peek, and picking an item submits ACTIVATE then
   DECLARE.
3. **Multi-step gestures, single-step engine.** The contract has `macro_moves=0`.
   A planned path is submitted one STEP at a time. The client stops at the first
   step that opens any other prompt (re-roll window, knockdown) and shows it.
4. **Surfaces.** `player` (click a token), `square` (click a lit square),
   `drag_player` (setup), `menu` (declare, stand up, end activation), `dialog`
   (dice, re-rolls, skills, apothecary, options), `button` (end turn, end setup).
   Each annotated action carries its surface.
5. **Human on the left.** The human's team is drawn attacking right (or upward on a
   phone) and red, the bot blue, regardless of home or away. Engine coordinates
   stay absolute; only the drawing mirrors. See open question 1.

### 3.2 Keyboard

Enter moves along the planned path, Esc clears it, 1/2/3 pick dice, R team
re-roll, H hides a dialog, Space pauses bot playback, F flags the last bot move,
M/B/Z/P/H/F/E declare letters as in FFB. Ctrl+T ends the turn (FFB binds it but
never handles it).

## 4. Model side

- **Recurrence.** `PolicySeat.step()` runs once per `c_step`, whoever decides.
  On waiting steps the support is the singleton (NONE, 32, 390) and the output is
  discarded, but the state advances. State is zeroed when the session starts a
  match and never mid-match. This is the native evaluation contract
  (`puffer_recurrent_eval_state.patch`): state persists across rollout calls and
  clears only on terminal rows. Training itself zeroes state every rollout
  (`reset_state=True`, horizon 64), so the evaluation contract, not the training
  one, is what the exams measured.
- **Kernel.** `native` reproduces the CUDA rollout kernel formulas (`fast_sigmoid`
  for negative candidates, branchy `lerp`). `torch` equals `pufferlib.models`
  exactly (test). On chain 25 the two differ by at most 3.1e-5 in logits over 600
  steps.
- **Sampling.** Default `sample`, as trained and examined, from exact joint
  support: type, then arg given type, then square given both. A toggle offers
  `argmax` (conditional argmax). Argmax is a different policy; games record the
  mode per game and results under the two modes are not pooled.
- **Think delay.** Inference takes about 1 ms on the Mac. The client paces bot
  moves for readability: 600 ms per board-changing decision, no delay for
  bookkeeping decisions (END_ACTIVATION, DECLINE_REROLL), pause and step
  controls. The delay is display-only and never changes what the policy sees.
- **Checkpoint choice.** `--checkpoint <blob>` with a mandatory `.lineage.json`
  (obs-v6, exact-joint-v1, 512x3, matching sha). Blobs are copied to
  `.play-artifacts/checkpoints/` (gitignored). CPU torch from the flat blob via
  `training/convert_checkpoint.cuda_to_torch`; biases zero-filled, which is exact.
- **Rosters.** Default procgen with the training skill-up settings (4 draws, up to
  2 skills, primary only). Forced team ids per side are supported. Seed and roster
  ids are recorded.
- **Why CPU torch and not a native CPU `_C`.** No obs-v6 exact-joint CPU `_C`
  exists on the Mac, the worktree has no vendored PufferLib, and a vec build is
  shaped for batched training rather than one env with a human in the loop. The
  shim compiles `bloodbowl.h` as one translation unit, so observations, masks,
  projection, `bbe_decode` and `c_step` are the training code; only the network
  forward is reimplemented, and that is pinned by tests and the parity plan.

## 5. Feedback loop

Per game `.play-artifacts/games/<stamp>/`:

- `game.json` (`bbplay-game-v1`): header (seed, episode, roster ids, human seat,
  policy provenance and mode, obs version), result, integrity counters, and a
  trace with one record per `c_step`: actor, engine action, policy tuple, state
  digest, policy value, policy log-probability on its decisions, forward index.
  The trace replays on a fresh engine to identical digests (`replay_trace`).
- `policy_logits.npz`: the 454 logits at every policy decision.
- Flags: `flag(note, step)` stores the step record, the pre-step `bb_match` bytes,
  the reasons and note, and links the logits. The panel shows the policy's top
  alternatives computed exactly over the support (the ACTIVATE and DECLARE
  decisions are joined into one line).
- Survey: strength (1..5), ball protection, stalling, free text for rules bugs.

Turning it into data:

- Diagnostics: flagged pre-states become probe positions. They come from legal
  play, so they satisfy the authored-state rule (no surgery). Re-running a new
  checkpoint on the same pre-state with the same seed shows whether the flagged
  choice changed.
- Training: Alex's own decisions export as BBP v4 pairs (exact conditional masks,
  canonical sentinels) for a later BC or KL-anchor study. Per AGENTS.md, outcomes
  are never used as action-quality labels, and flags are diagnostics, not labels.
- Survey answers roll up per checkpoint as a human-perceived strength log beside
  the tournament numbers, never in place of them.

## 6. Architecture

```
browser client (planned)            play server (planned, 127.0.0.1:8790)
  pitch canvas/SVG, dialogs  <--ws-->  GameSession (play_harness/session.py)
  replay + flag panel                    |  Engine (engine.py, ctypes)
                                         |    libbbplay: bloodbowl.h single TU
                                         |  PolicySeat (policy.py, CPU torch)
                                         |  recorder -> .play-artifacts/games
```

The server is new code, not an extension of `stream_backend/server.py`: that
server broadcasts one shared match to many viewers and ignores input. It reuses
BBTV drawing conventions, skill names and the icon mapping.

Websocket messages (JSON, `{"v":1,"t":...}`):

- client to server: `new_game {checkpoint, human_team, bot_team, human_seat,
  seed, mode, think_ms}`, `submit {state_version, action_id}`,
  `submit_path {state_version, squares[]}`, `peek {state_version, action_id}`,
  `control {pause|step|speed}`, `flag {step, reasons[], note}`,
  `survey {answers}`, `resync`.
- server to client: `hello`, `state` (session.state()), `legal`
  (session.legal()), `events {applied[]}` for animation, `bot_move {record,
  alternatives[]}`, `over {result}`, `error {reason, state_version}`.

Session JSON API as built: `state()`, `legal()` (prompt, annotated actions,
groups by type), `submit()`, `flag()`, `survey()`, `save()`, `bot_suggestion()`
for tests. One session per browser tab; one process may hold several sessions.

## 8. Backend spike status

Built:

- `play_harness/native/bbplay.c`: ctypes shim. Mirrors `apply_kwargs` for every
  field that changes observations, legality, rosters or episode bounds (drift
  guard test parses `binding.c`). Validates tuples before `c_step`, rebuilds the
  natural final state after the env's auto-reset, exposes state digest, contact
  bot, step odds, reach field, block EV, names, and `bbp_peek_legal`. Built with
  `-ffp-contract=off` so float-derived observation bytes are not fused on arm64.
- `engine.py`, `policy.py`, `session.py`, `drivers.py`, `parity.py`,
  `bench_vs_bot.py`.

Test evidence (`.venv/bin/python -m pytest play_harness/tests`, 4 threads):
21 passed, 1 skipped (the rig fixture test, no fixture yet).

- 10 full matches with chain 25 on the policy seat, human seat alternating HOME
  and AWAY, coverage and contact-bot drivers through JSON round trips: all
  natural completions (status MATCH_OVER, 471 to 1281 c-steps), zero illegal,
  projection collision, error, engine rejection and API rejection counters, and
  policy forwards equal to c-steps in every match. About 0.5 to 1.4 s per match.
- 40 random-vs-coverage matches for rare windows: all natural, zero counters, every
  action type presented at least 3 times was submitted from the human seat.
- Recurrence: the state changes on every waiting-coach forward; state is zero only
  before the first forward; replaying the recorded observations every step
  reproduces the session's logits exactly, while stepping only on the policy's
  own decisions changes them (the D388 failure mode, as a control).
- Determinism: same seed and same human actions reproduce the trace (digests,
  tuples, log-probabilities); a recorded trace replays on a fresh engine; a
  different sampling seed changes the trace.
- Exact joint selection never leaves support over 600 windows x 8 samples, and
  its log-probability matches the stored masks.

Bench, descriptive only (chain 25 vs contact bot, 100 games per seat, CPU,
native kernel): see section 8.1.

### 8.1 Bench numbers

Chain 25 (sha `109c55d3`, native kernel, sampling) against the contact bot,
seeds 1..100 per seat, procgen rosters, kickoff starts. Every game completed
naturally with zero integrity counters. Artifacts in `.play-artifacts/bench/`.

| Arm | Seat | W / D / L | TD for / against per game | W + D/2 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| every-step (harness contract) | HOME | 34 / 37 / 29 | 0.56 / 0.50 | 0.525 |
| every-step (harness contract) | AWAY | 26 / 46 / 28 | 0.41 / 0.43 | 0.490 |
| learner-only (D388 bridge bug) | HOME | 21 / 53 / 26 | 0.43 / 0.49 | 0.475 |
| learner-only (D388 bridge bug) | AWAY | 17 / 56 / 27 | 0.31 / 0.47 | 0.450 |

Descriptive screening on 100 games per cell, not a confidence interval. The

they neither confirm nor refute parity.

## 9. Recurrence parity plan

1. **Kernel parity (done).** Torch kernel equals `pufferlib.models` bit for bit;
   native-kernel formulas differ from it by at most 3.1e-5 on chain 25 logits.
2. **Rig fixture (to record).** When the rig GPU is free, run a small fp32 native
   evaluation (horizon 1, 2 agents, `set_evaluation_mode(True)`, contact bot on
   the other seat, 2 matches) through `tools/puffer_cuda_runtime.py` and dump
   `qualification_snapshot` after each rollout. Write it as
   `bbplay-parity-fixture-v1` (format in `play_harness/parity.py`): per forward
   the policy-row obs, a reset flag (terminal delivered with this obs, or t = 0),
   deciding flag, the rewritten conditional mask row, the sampled tuple, native
   log-probability and value, the tuple `c_step` applied, and optional logits;
   manifest with checkpoint and module sha, `precision_bytes = 4`, per-env seed
   (vec base seed + env index) and episode index.
3. **Checks.** `BBPLAY_PARITY_FIXTURE=<dir> BBPLAY_CHECKPOINT=<blob> pytest
   play_harness/tests/test_parity_fixture.py` rebuilds the match on the Mac shim
   and requires byte-identical observations (this also proves the arm64 build
   encodes like the rig's gcc build), then replays the observation stream through
   the torch policy with the recorded resets and requires log-probability and
   value within 1e-4. Run with both kernels and record which one matches.
   A self-recorded fixture already exercises the whole path in CI.
4. **Behavioral parity.** `bench_vs_bot.py` at exam scale (both seats, same roster
   distribution) compared with the native exam cells for chain 25, reading
   distributions only because torch and curand sample differently. The
   `--learner-only` arm is the control that should reproduce the D388 drop.

## 10. UX critique and changes

Adversarial pass against FFB conventions and learnability, after looking at every
render:

| Finding | Change |
|---|---|
| Prone tokens were a smudge with the position hidden under the slash | lying token keeps the position text and carries a DOWN or STUN tag |
| Tackle-zone tint competed with the path | lowered to 11% per zone, capped at 30% |
| Clock only in the rail | clock added to the scoreboard centre |
| Block dialog covered the bottom of the pitch and pushed past the frame | narrower dialog, clamped inside the pitch, H hides it |
| "Block saves you" was wrong for a Big Un without Block | outcome lines follow the actual skills: "Both fall · turnover" |
| A "Keep dice" button duplicated picking a die | removed; picking a die declines the re-roll |
| Explainer paragraphs ("Your dice stay on screen...", "At 0 only End setup remains...") | removed; the budget number stays, the rule goes to a tooltip |
| Invalid setup squares were invisible under red tokens | the whole offending wide zone is tinted |
| Wide zones named top and bottom, which flips on the upright phone pitch | named left and right from the coach's facing, which holds in both orientations |
| Policy alternative labels were white text over a light track | label above a thin bar, percentage right-aligned |
| "Bot turn" banner in the human's red | bot activity uses the bot's blue |
| Phone layout overflowed on the flag screen | layout class with a phone override |

Basin check: texture on the frame and pitch, asymmetric rails (bot rail carries
different content), no single-accent dark dashboard, no Cinzel or mono data font.
The risk taken: dialogs anchored on the pitch instead of FFB's centred modals, and
the human always drawn on the left.

## 11. Risks and open questions

Risks:

- Parity is unverified against native until the rig fixture exists. The shim
  build could encode float-derived obs bytes differently from the rig.
- Several optional skills are engine-automatic, so Alex will sometimes want a
  choice the engine does not offer. The harness logs it; the fix is engine work.
- Human games are few and not held out, so survey strength is anecdote beside
  tournaments.

Open questions for Alex:

1. Always draw you on the left in red even when you are the away team, or follow
   FFB and draw home on the left?
2. Default roster: random procgen as trained, or pick both teams each game?
